Cold statics steps on the build server, 2026-11-14.

Question: in the headless Unreal runs a statics step took on average 2-3 times longer than in the core bench,
with spikes up to 25 ms. Is it Unreal?

Method: the scenarios of the Unreal project now live in the core (core/include/ssproto/Scenario.h) and count in
world steps, so the core bench and Unreal destroy the same pieces in the same steps; the traces (FNV-1a of every
step result) must be equal. The core bench runs them in three modes: steps back to back; a sleep of 50 ms before
every step (the rhythm of a game: the CPU idles between world steps); a busy wait of 50 ms (the CPU never idles).
Unreal: UnrealEditor.exe -server (no window, at most 30 frames a second), prototype\ue5\check_headless.ps1.
Machine: Ryzen 7 9700X, Windows Server 2025, power plan Balanced (minimum processor state 5 %).

Answer: no. All 8 traces of Unreal equal the core bench, and the same steps back to back inside Unreal take
about as long as in the core bench (large grenades: 176.5 ms against 156.4 ms). A step is slow when the CPU idled
before it ("cold"): the core alone with a sleep of 50 ms takes 1175.9 ms for the same steps (the heaviest step 48 ms,
0.6 ms back to back), with a busy wait 324.2 ms; Unreal in the rhythm of the game (wakes every 33 ms) 413.2 ms.
Spikes of 10-20 ms remain even with a busy wait; probably other processes of this server preempt the thread.
